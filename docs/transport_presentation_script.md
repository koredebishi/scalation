# VMT Tax Presentation Script

## Presentation Flow Guide
**Total Slides:** 10 slides  
**Estimated Time:** 15-20 minutes  
**Structure:** 3 Questions → Evidence → Conclusion

---

## SLIDE 1: Title + Three Questions

**Opening:**
"Good morning/afternoon. Today I'm presenting on mileage-based transportation funding, specifically the Vehicle Miles Traveled tax or VMT. This presentation is organized around three critical questions that policymakers and the public need to answer."

**The Three Questions:**
1. Should transportation funding be based on how much you drive?
2. Would mileage-based fees disproportionately affect rural or low-income drivers?
3. How should privacy concerns be addressed if mileage tracking is required?

**Transition:** "Let's dive into the first question."

---

## QUESTION 1: Should funding be based on miles driven?

### SLIDE 2: Why VMT?

**Key Points to Say:**
- "The average US driver travels 13 to 14 thousand miles per year according to the Federal Highway Administration."
- "Oregon is currently piloting VMT for 5,000 vehicles because fuel tax revenue is declining."
- "The main reason: fuel tax doesn't capture electric vehicles."
- "**The key advantage of VMT:** All vehicles would contribute - including EVs. This creates a more sustainable and fair funding model."

**Pause for emphasis:** "But does VMT make sense given how differently people drive across the country?"

---

### SLIDE 3: State Variations

**Key Points to Say:**
- "Driving patterns vary dramatically by state."
- "Wyoming residents drive over 21,000 miles per year - the highest in the nation."
- "Compare that to Washington DC at only 6,700 miles - less than a third."
- "Here in Georgia, we average 17,500 miles per year."
- "That means a Georgia resident drives twice as much as a New York resident and three times as much as a DC resident."

**Main Point:** "A VMT tax better reflects actual road usage. You pay for what you use, regardless of what you drive."

**Transition:** "So the answer to Question 1 is Yes - VMT is a more equitable and sustainable funding model. But this raises Question 2..."

---

## QUESTION 2: Would this hurt rural or low-income drivers?

### SLIDE 4: Urban vs Rural Impact

**Opening:**
"This is the most common objection: 'VMT will hurt rural residents who have to drive more.' But the research tells a different story."

**Key Points to Say:**
- "Yes, rural residents do drive more miles."
- "Yes, they also tend to drive less fuel-efficient vehicles - trucks, SUVs."
- "But here's the key: **They already pay MORE under the current fuel tax system.**"
- "VMT actually **reduces** the relative difference between what rural and urban residents pay."

**Cite Evidence:**
- "McMullen and colleagues in 2010 found that rural households actually **benefit more** from switching to VMT."
- "Schroeckenthaler and Fitzroy in 2019 confirmed that urban areas would pay slightly more under VMT."

**Main Point:** "The claim that VMT hurts rural residents doesn't hold up to scrutiny."

---

### SLIDE 5: Low vs High Income Impact

**Opening:**
"What about low-income drivers? My initial concern was that this would burden them more."

**Acknowledge the concern:**
- "It's true that middle and upper-class workers work remotely more often."
- "Blue-collar workers typically must commute to work sites."
- "So it seems like VMT would hit them harder."

**But the research contradicts this:**
- "Yes, VMT is regressive - it takes a larger percentage of low-income budgets."
- "**But** - and this is critical - the fuel tax is **MORE** regressive."
- "Why? Higher-income households own newer, more fuel-efficient vehicles."
- "They drive the same or more miles but pay less in fuel tax."

**Main Point:** "VMT actually **reduces** tax regressivity compared to the current system. Weatherford's 2011 study confirmed that VMT shifts the tax burden from low-income to high-income households."

---

### SLIDE 6: Public Perception vs Reality

**Opening:**
"So why do people think VMT is unfair if the research shows otherwise?"

**Public Opinion Data:**
- "The National Academies analyzed 38 surveys on VMT."
- "Only 33 to 45 percent say VMT is a fair way to raise revenue."
- "Even fewer - only 15 to 38 percent - say it's fairer than fuel tax."
- "People say it's not fair for rural residents, long-distance drivers, and work commuters."

**The Gap:**
"But here's the problem - and I quote from the research: **'These opinions are inconsistent with the empirical findings.'**"

**The Good News:**
- "General public support: only 24 percent."
- "But pilot program participants - people who actually experienced VMT: 51 percent support."
- "**That's more than double.** Understanding increases support."

**Main Point:** "So the answer to Question 2 is No - VMT does not disproportionately hurt rural or low-income drivers. In fact, it helps them compared to fuel tax. The problem is public perception, not reality."

**Transition:** "This brings us to the final question - the one that concerns people most."

---

## QUESTION 3: How do we address privacy concerns?

### SLIDE 7: Privacy Concerns Are Real

**Opening:**
"Privacy concerns are valid and must be taken seriously."

**The Data:**
- "National Academies analyzed 38 surveys, but only 4 asked about privacy."
- "When asked, more than 50 percent of respondents expressed concern about location tracking."
- "The main objection: knowing **where and when** you drive."

**The Key Finding:**
"But here's the important part: **Odometer-only data did not generate undue concern.**"

**Main Point:** "The issue isn't tracking miles - it's tracking location. If we can measure miles without tracking location, the privacy concern largely disappears."

---

### SLIDE 8: Privacy Solutions

**Opening:**
"So how do we address this?"

**Three Solutions:**
1. **"Use odometer-only collection - no GPS, no location data, no timestamps. Just total miles driven."**
2. **"Have a private firm collect the data, not the government directly."**
3. **"Minimize data collection to only what's essential."**

**Context - We Already Accept This:**
- "Consider what we already use: E-ZPass tracks when you pass toll plazas."
- "OnStar in GM vehicles tracks your location and driving."
- "Insurance apps like Allstate DriveWise and Progressive Snapshot track when, where, and how you drive."
- "Your cell phone is tracked by private and public entities constantly."

**Main Point:** "We already accept tracking for convenience and cost savings. VMT can be implemented with **less** invasive tracking than what we use daily."

**Conclusion for Question 3:** "Privacy concerns are valid but solvable. Odometer-only collection with private data handling addresses the main objections."

---

## SLIDE 9: Summary - Answering All Three Questions

**Opening:**
"Let me directly answer the three questions we started with."

**Question 1: Should transportation funding be based on miles driven?**
- "**Yes.** It includes all vehicles including EVs, creates sustainable funding, and reflects actual road usage."

**Question 2: Would this hurt rural or low-income drivers?**
- "**No.** Research shows VMT actually reduces inequity compared to fuel tax - contrary to public perception."

**Question 3: How do we address privacy?**
- "**Odometer-only tracking with private collection.** No location or time data needed."

**The Big Challenge:**
"The key challenge is education. Public perception doesn't match research findings. As we saw, when people actually experience VMT in pilot programs, support more than doubles."

**Closing Point:** "Transparency and education are critical to implementation success."

---

**Say:**
"All of my evidence comes from peer-reviewed research and government reports, primarily synthesized in David Sjoquist's 2023 report from Georgia State University's Center for State and Local Finance, along with studies from the National Academies, Congressional Budget Office, and academic researchers."

**Optional:** "I'm happy to take questions."

## Q&A Preparation

### Anticipated Questions:

**Q: How would VMT be collected?**
A: Annual odometer checks (like emissions inspections), or voluntary electronic reporting. Oregon's pilot uses both options.

**Q: What about people who drive across state lines?**
A: This is a legitimate implementation challenge. Solutions include state compacts (like E-ZPass) or federal coordination. Oregon's pilot only charges for in-state miles.

**Q: Won't this hurt people in rural areas who have no alternative?**
A: They already pay more under fuel tax because they drive larger, less efficient vehicles. VMT reduces - not increases - their relative burden.

**Q: What's the rate per mile?**
A: Oregon pilots around 1.8 cents per mile. The rate would be set to be revenue-neutral initially - same total revenue as current fuel tax.

**Q: When will this happen?**
A: Still in pilot phase. Oregon, Utah, and several other states are testing. Full implementation likely 5-10 years away, particularly as EV adoption accelerates the funding crisis.

**Q: What about vehicle weight? Heavy vehicles damage roads more.**
A: Valid point. VMT could be adjusted by vehicle weight class, similar to commercial truck fees. This is more feasible with VMT than fuel tax.

## Presentation Tips

### Tone:
- **Objective and evidence-based** - you're presenting research findings, not advocating
- Acknowledge when initial assumptions were wrong (low-income impact)
- Emphasize the gap between perception and reality

### Body Language:
- Make eye contact during key statistics
- Pause after "but" statements (e.g., "BUT fuel tax is MORE regressive")
- Use hand gestures for comparisons (2x, 3x differences)

### Emphasis Points:
1. **"All vehicles contribute"** - including EVs
2. **"VMT reduces inequity"** - not increases it
3. **"Odometer-only"** - no location tracking needed
4. **"Public perception doesn't match research"** - this is the real challenge

### If Running Long:
- Skip detailed state mileage comparisons (Slide 3)
- Condense public opinion data (Slide 6)
- Keep Q1, Q2 core equity slides, and Q3 solutions

### If Running Short:
- Expand on pilot program details
- Discuss implementation challenges in more depth
- Provide more context on why fuel tax is failing

---

## Key Takeaway for Audience

**"VMT is more equitable, more sustainable, and privacy-compliant - but only if we educate the public on what research actually shows versus what people believe."**
