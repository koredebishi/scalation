% Slide 2
\begin{frame}{Prompt 1: Central Questions}
\large

\vspace{0.5em}

\begin{itemize}
\item Is securing health data a life and death matter?
\vspace{0.5em}
\item If cyberattacks disrupt care, should healthcare cybersecurity be treated as a public safety problem rather than an IT problem?
\vspace{0.5em}
\item What happens if we continue to treat healthcare cyber incidents as technical failures instead of risks to human life?
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% Slide 3
\begin{frame}{Prompt 2: Patient Deaths and Evidence}
\rider{The numbers suggest harm, but official attribution still stays at zero.}

\vspace{0.5em}

\begin{itemize}
\item Do you believe that cyberattacks on healthcare organizations can increase patient mortality?
\vspace{0.5em}
\item If the statistics strongly correlate ransomware with mortality during hospital cyberattacks, why is the death report still at zero, especially with the survey saying 53\% mortality rate?
\vspace{0.5em}
\item If the evidence is this strong, why does the system still seem reluctant to say that people may already be dying from these attacks?
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% Slide 4
\begin{frame}{Prompt 3: Critical Infrastructure}
\rider{The article raises a basic but unsettling question: if Change was this important, why did the government not already know it?}

\vspace{0.5em}

\begin{itemize}
\item Is the government doing enough to protect critical companies like Change?
\vspace{0.5em}
\item If Change was this important, why was it not already treated that way before the hack?
\vspace{0.5em}
\item What happens if there are other unknown single points of failure in health care that are still unprotected?
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% Slide 5
\begin{frame}{Prompt 4: Regulation and Capacity}
\rider{Rules may be necessary, but rules alone may not work.}

\vspace{0.5em}

\begin{itemize}
\item Should health care companies be forced to up their cybersecurity?
\vspace{0.5em}
\item You can push as many requirements on hospitals as you want, but if you do not provide them with funding, staffing, and expertise, are those requirements really fair?
\vspace{0.5em}
\item Is it fair to fine hospitals for weak cybersecurity when some hospitals, especially rural hospitals, may need help more than punishment?
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% Slide 6
\begin{frame}{Prompt 5: Scale and Systemic Risk}
\rider{Corporate size and national-level vulnerability.}

\vspace{0.5em}

\begin{itemize}
\item Is "UnitedHealth" too big to protect?
\vspace{0.5em}
\item When a private company becomes a single point of failure, does it become a public responsibility?
\vspace{0.5em}
\item What happens when efficiency through consolidation reduces system resilience?
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% Slide 7
\begin{frame}{Prompt 6: Ransomware Policy}
\rider{Trade-off between rapid recovery and long-term risk.}

\vspace{0.5em}

\begin{itemize}
\item Is it time to ban ransom payments?
\vspace{0.5em}
\item Should ransom payments be banned even if doing so may delay recovery and risk patient lives?
\vspace{0.5em}
\item What happens if hospitals must choose between paying criminals or delaying care?
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% Slide 8
\begin{frame}{Prompt 7: Final Takeaway}
\rider{Both articles push the same point: this is no longer just about protecting data. It is about patient care, public responsibility, and what happens when systems fail.}

\vspace{0.5em}

\begin{itemize}
\item Has healthcare cybersecurity become too important to leave to private incentives, voluntary standards, and after-the-fact damage control?
\vspace{0.5em}
\item What does government owe hospitals before the next attack happens?
\vspace{0.5em}
\item After both readings, what matters most: prevention, accountability, or resilience?
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}
