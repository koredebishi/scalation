# ANNSIM 2027 plan (settled 2026-11-09, written 2026-11-10)

Status: plan only. No results yet. Korede will come back to this.

## The paper

The IDM-derived meso link against micro on a real congested corridor:
I-210 Westbound, Pasadena, Sierra Madre Villa to Hill (S1-S4).

Four configurations:

1. micro (`model/i210w/I210W.scala`)
2. mixed (`model/i210w/I210W_withMeso.scala`)
3. meso, IDM-derived diagram (`model/i210w/I210W_fullMeso.scala`, `dynamics/IdmFlux.scala`)
4. meso, fitted speed-density diagram with a minimum speed (Greenshields or Burghout form) - NOT STARTED

The question the paper answers: does deriving the meso diagram from the micro IDM lose
anything against a fitted diagram that carries two or more calibrated numbers, and does
micro earn its cost at a real bottleneck.

## Why Pasadena

The PI wanted a real corridor, not a toy. Pasadena has a recurrent jam head at Lake,
5-minute PeMS bins, a 17-hour day (06:00-23:00, 205 bins), three on-ramps and three off-ramps.
Calibrate on 2024-12-03, hold out 2024-12-10 and 2024-12-17.

New code meant for this paper: one corridor class (done), one loader (done), one
fitted-diagram class behind the `speed(rho)` / `q_max` interface of `IdmFlux` (not started).

## What changed on 2026-11-09

- Corridor and the three runs are done ahead of plan (10 reps, 2024-12-03, outputs in
  `log/simulation/pads_reps/i210w/2024-12-03/`).
- New item, a prerequisite: the free sink at Hill. The queue that makes Pasadena
  interesting starts at Lake 1 (VDS 717634), outside the modelled stretch. S2-S4 speeds are
  too fast 09-11 h and 15-19 h in all three models. The downstream speed boundary
  (phantom leader at the sink, Treiber-Hennecke-Helbing 2000 D15 recipe) has to be in the
  model before any of the four configurations can be judged. Design in memory note
  `pasadena-boundary-open`; not approved, not coded.
- The fitted-diagram class is not started.
