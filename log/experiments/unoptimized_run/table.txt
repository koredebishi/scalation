\subsection{Shifted Erlang-2 Arrivals}

Once vehicle dynamics were integrated using \textbf{Algorithm~1}, the remaining challenge was generating vehicle arrivals at realistic inter-arrival rates. Vehicle arrivals are commonly modeled using exponential inter-arrival times, corresponding to a Poisson process \cite{law2007simulation}. However, exponential distributions permit arbitrarily small headways, which is physically unrealistic due to finite vehicle length and driver reaction constraints.

Shifted headway distributions address this limitation by enforcing a minimum inter-arrival time \cite{cowan1975useful}. Following this approach, we employ a shifted Erlang-2 distribution, which preserves the smoothness of the Erlang family while enforcing a hard lower bound on headway.

\textbf{Variate generation.} Let $U_1, U_2 \sim \text{Uniform}(0,1)$ be independent uniform variates, $\mu > 0$ the exponential stage mean, and $\tau > 0$ the minimum headway. An Erlang-2 variable is the sum of two exponentials: $X = E_1 + E_2$ with $E_i = -\mu \ln(U_i)$, giving $X = -\mu \ln(U_1 U_2)$. Adding the shift $\tau$ yields the headway:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:erlang-variate}
Y = \tau - \mu \ln(U_1 U_2), \quad \text{with mean } \mathbb{E}[Y] = \tau + 2\mu.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Data-driven parameterization.}
The mean inter-arrival time for lane $\ell$ during interval $t$ is $\bar{\mu}_{\ell,t} = \Delta T / N_{\ell,t}$, where $\Delta T = 900$ seconds and $N_{\ell,t}$ is the observed count. Setting $\mathbb{E}[Y] = \bar{\mu}_{\ell,t}$ and solving:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:erlang-param}
\mu = \frac{\bar{\mu}_{\ell,t} - \tau}{2}.
\end{equation}
